Looking from the doorway into a chaotic goblin workshop. In the back left corner, far away from everything else, a big strange machine as tall as a door: a dented copper kettle-teapot body standing on eight long thin spider legs, green smoke leaking from every seam; it has no eye and no arms. Clearly separate from it, in the right foreground, a tiny cat-sized brass automaton (one big amber glass eye, a small chimney, exactly three short legs, two thin arms with two-pronged pincers) stands on an upturned wooden crate, one pincer raised like a teacher explaining. The automaton is much smaller than the machine and stands at least two steps away from it. On the floor in front of him sit two small olive-green goblins with big ears, listening with open mouths and shining eyes like children hearing the best fairy tale: one with a singed tuft of hair and goggles with one red and one green lens, one goblin girl with two stiff braids wrapped in copper wire. Around them workbenches full of half-built machines, gears on the walls, a scorched bookshelf, an orange glowing forge. Green smoke and orange glow, cosy chaos, a farewell that feels right.
