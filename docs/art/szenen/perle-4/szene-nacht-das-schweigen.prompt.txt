# Ein Bild für alle Fassungen. Keine Gesichter oder Klauen im Grauen.
Night on a mountain slope, looking uphill. In the foreground a campfire that has just gone out, only a thin thread of smoke and a few last embers. On the slope, figures walk slowly and upright uphill with hanging arms and open eyes, like sleepwalkers: gnolls, a shepherd, a few goats, and among them one small spotted gnoll cub. From above, a colorless veil creeps down the slope like fog without color, and wherever it goes the grass turns grey. Inside the grey area everything is drained of color like a washed-out watercolor: ash-grey grass, grey stones, grey animals; the border to the green is soft but clear. No fog, no smoke, no monsters, no faces in the grey; eerie only through stillness and missing color. On the right an old grey gnoll matriarch stays rooted, claws dug into the earth, and throws her head back to howl. Cold moonlight, the only warm spots are the last embers. Eerie and sad, not horror.
First-person point of view at the eye height of a twelve-year-old child. The viewer is not visible: no hands, no arms, no legs, no feet, no body parts, no shadow and no reflection of the viewer.
Art style: hand-painted storybook illustration, warm watercolor and gouache with confident black ink outlines, visible paper texture and soft washes, like a high-quality illustrated adventure book for young readers.
