# Die Magie geht vom Betrachter aus, ohne Hände.
A wave as tall as a house rises out of the foaming mill race at the foot of a burning wooden drying tower, arches over the tower and crashes down on the flames; huge clouds of white steam, sparkling drops, a deep blue glow in the water. The magic comes from the viewer. High on the platform at the top, small but visible, a twelve-year-old girl with chestnut curls (tied back with a dark-green ribbon) escaping from under a flat cap, freckles, hazel eyes, wearing a far too big brown worker's jacket with rolled-up sleeves over a dark-green velvet jacket with small gold buttons that peeks out, and fine leather shoes with silver buckles, drenched and coughing. Workers, smiths and kobolds stare. The first awakening of magic: overwhelming, beautiful, a little frightening.
First-person point of view at the eye height of a twelve-year-old child. The viewer is not visible: no hands, no arms, no legs, no feet, no body parts, no shadow and no reflection of the viewer.
Art style: hand-painted storybook illustration, warm watercolor and gouache with confident black ink outlines, visible paper texture and soft washes, like a high-quality illustrated adventure book for young readers. Full-bleed scene, no border. No text, no letters, no labels, no numbers, no signature.
