Standing on a gravel road in the evening, looking east up a long valley towards a huge grey mountain wall. A narrow deep gorge cuts into it, and across the gorge lies a grey stone fortress with three towers and a black iron gate; on the highest tower a round mirror flashes in the low evening sun like a giant eye. On the road ahead a long, silent line of people walks towards the fortress: young and old, some with bundles on their backs, each with a small brass plaque on a string around the neck. Riders in long black coats with golden balance-scale breastplates ride before and after them. Long shadows, golden light on the peaks. Awe and unease. The line of registered people walks away from the viewer towards the fortress, seen from behind; the black-coated mercenaries ride complete horses with all four legs visible, and no animal part merges with any person.
First-person point of view at the eye height of a twelve-year-old child. The viewer is not visible: no hands, no arms, no legs, no feet, no body parts, no shadow and no reflection of the viewer.
The painted scene fills the center; towards the edges it gradually dissolves into loose pencil sketch lines, unfinished ink hatching and splashes of watercolor on cream paper.
Art style: hand-painted storybook illustration, warm watercolor and gouache with confident black ink outlines, visible paper texture and soft washes, like a high-quality illustrated adventure book for young readers. Full-bleed scene, no border. No text, no letters, no labels, no numbers, no signature.
