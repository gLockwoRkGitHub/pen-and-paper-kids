# Das Kind sieht den Betrachter an. Die Kuppel ist durchsichtig.
Evening in the torch-lit fortress courtyard, standing among the registered mages and looking up at a wooden grandstand. On it a tall slender woman about forty in a black high-collared gown with a heavy golden chain and a small balance-scale pendant rests one gloved hand on the shoulder of a twelve-year-old boy with short, slightly unruly chestnut curls, freckles and hazel eyes, dressed in a dark-green velvet jacket with small gold buttons (one button missing on the sleeve), white shirt and polished shoes with silver buckles. The child stands stiff and straight and looks directly at the viewer with a pained expression. Behind them a translucent dome of light, thin as soap-bubble skin and shimmering in brown, blue, red and white, arches over the whole fortress, rising from the great mirror on the tower. In the foreground the backs of mages with their hands on bronze plates, some sinking to their knees. Splendor and betrayal.
First-person point of view at the eye height of a twelve-year-old child. The viewer is not visible: no hands, no arms, no legs, no feet, no body parts, no shadow and no reflection of the viewer.
The painted scene fills the center; towards the edges it gradually dissolves into loose pencil sketch lines, unfinished ink hatching and splashes of watercolor on cream paper.
Art style: hand-painted storybook illustration, warm watercolor and gouache with confident black ink outlines, visible paper texture and soft washes, like a high-quality illustrated adventure book for young readers. Full-bleed scene, no border. No text, no letters, no labels, no numbers, no signature.
