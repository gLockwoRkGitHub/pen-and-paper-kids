A single small sketch drawn by a twelve-year-old in a travel diary: charcoal pencil with a little watercolor, lively but slightly clumsy lines, a few smudges. It sits on plain, slightly yellowed cream paper with a wide empty margin all around: no book, no page edges, no table, no shadow, nothing else on the paper. The sketch shows a small square patch of dark cloth with loose threads at the edges. On the patch a tiny flat emblem is stitched in pale thread: a simple outline of an upright finger in front of a curved line like closed lips, the guild sign of the Leise Hand. Only the cloth patch, nothing else: no person, no real hand, no arm, no jacket, no fingers holding the patch. No writing, no letters, no words, no numbers anywhere.
