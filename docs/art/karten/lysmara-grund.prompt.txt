A hand-drawn town plan of a harbor city, seen from straight above. On the left the sea with a round harbor bay and a thick quay wall along the bay. A wide river comes from the upper right corner, runs down along the right side of the town and then along the bottom to the sea at the lower left. An old town wall drawn as a thick line encloses the town in a rough oval. A few main streets as simple lines: one from the harbor up to a rectangular empty square in the middle, one from the square up to a hill in the upper part of the town, one from the square to a gate on the right, one from the square up to a gate at the top left. Between the streets many small simple rooftops seen from above, densest near the harbor, larger houses with little gardens on the hill. The square in the middle stays empty. No towers, no cranes, no ships, no water wheels, no bridges, no fountain, no special buildings, no people.
Art style: hand-drawn travel-diary map, charcoal pencil and black ink lines with a little soft watercolor (pale blue water, grey-brown mountains, very pale green grass strokes), lively but slightly clumsy lines, as drawn by a twelve-year-old who draws well, matching a hand-painted storybook. On a plain pure white background: no paper texture, no stains, no shadows, no border, no compass rose, no decorations. No text, no letters, no labels, no numbers, no signature.
